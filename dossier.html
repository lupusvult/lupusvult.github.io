About this Website

It's been a long time since I last ran a website. Back then, discovering a new site was fun. They were primitive and personal, designed and built from scratch, and free of bloat, aside from the occasional random GIF floating around, of course.

After the rapid rise of content management systems, people started deploying them, slapping on a theme, and calling it a day. At the same time, social media platforms took over and killed bulletin board community culture. But it didn't stop there. JavaScript was on the rise, it enabled tracking by advertising companies and three-letter agencies. The internet wasn't the good old Wild West anymore. It felt like it lost its character, and I lost my interest.

I came up with the idea for this website on April 16, 2026, while browsing featured Neocities websites. They reminded me how happy I used to be designing and maintaining websites and moderating communities when I was younger.

Purpose

This site is a personal archive of ideas and data, my own little cozy space. My non-sensitive notes and spreadsheets are scattered everywhere, so this gives me a way to bring them all under one roof.

The internet has become too centralized. Social media platforms censor speech, whether due to government pressure or advertiser interests. Personal websites, on the other hand, are ungovernable. You can write whatever you want, and if a provider takes it down, you can simply move it elsewhere without losing a single bit. So instead of posting on social media platforms, I maintain a page where I share what I'm currently up to, updating it whenever life gets interesting and I remember to update it.

Design Choices

I've tried to keep everything ultra-minimalist while capturing the soul of Y2K-era personal websites. It's a static website, and the entire structure branches out from the main page.

Before building it, I set a few rules and stuck to them:

  1. It should feel raw, yet visually appealing.
  2. Must have some character.
  3. No web fonts.
  4. Keep CSS clean, no unused code.
  5. Pages must stay under 100 KB.
  6. Must be stateless, no cookies.
  7. Avoid JavaScript entirely.
  8. No analytics, even privacy-friendly ones.

End Goal

I want to optimize the shit out of this website, make it as small and fast as possible without sacrificing anything, and run it until the end of time. It can evolve over time, new pages might appear out of nowhere if I pick up a new hobby.

Ground Rules

The existence of this list might sound a little harsh, or even extreme, but it's not as bad as it may seem in reality. Nobody is forced to fit within them, we simply don't have to interact if our boundaries or expectations are incompatible. I don't expect more from others than what I'm willing to give myself, so I think it's pretty fair.

This list was forged through experiences, and I have a low tolerance level because of it. Doing any of the following will result in an instagib, even if the person did it as a joke.

  1. I don't lend money, because people's behavior changes when you ask for it back. I also don't tolerate people trying to get money out of me or profit from my work without making a fully transparent and fair deal first.
  2. If something needs to be done, it should be done immediately. When someone deliberately drags their feet on something that involves or affects me, it bothers me. It feels like they're dumping their responsibilities on me and using me to pick up the slack.
  3. It doesn't matter how public or unimportant the information is; I don't like people giving information about me to someone else. The correct response is, "None of my business. Go ask him yourself" and leave it at that.
  4. Everyone has problems. When someone starts whining about theirs, it makes me think about the problems I'm dealing with at that moment, and time just passes while nothing gets solved. If it's not something I can actually do anything about, I'd rather not hear it. It's mentally draining.
  5. I don't care what kind of lie it is; they're not acceptable. Secrets are in the same basket. If someone knows something that will affect me in one way or another, I expect to know about it.
  6. Promising or speaking on my behalf is not acceptable. If something involves my time, work, or participation, I expect to be asked directly, without a third party getting involved.
  7. When I say I don't want to do something, I mean it. My mind isn't going to change. Asking me the same thing multiple times might seem polite to some people, but to me, it's just annoying.
  8. I don't like unpredictable situations, and I don't do things without properly planning. If someone knocks on my door without letting me know beforehand, they won't be coming in.
  9. It's better not to promise anything than to make a promise they can't keep. Plans can be made based on those promises, so breaking them can cause a domino effect.
  10. Our opinions, small-scale personal observations, and emotions mean nothing when they're presented as facts. Research and data on the subject come first. I lose my shit when people talk about something as if it's a fact without having any proof to back it up.
  11. I don't care how people devour their food, but behaviors like smacking their lips, talking with food in their mouth, and licking their fingers are disgusting and disrespectful.
